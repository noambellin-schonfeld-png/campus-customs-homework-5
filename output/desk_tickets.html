Workflow

What could be simpler as one agent with tools?

Ticket 102 is the clearest single-agent case. One Lease Desk agent could read ticket 102, call get_lease_status, compare the due date with desk.date_today, check get_cash_accounts and pending approvals, then prepare the rent request and board note. That is a short chain with no inventory or customer-message question, and the actual Boss ended up coordinating Facilities and Accounting before preparing the request anyway. It would reduce delegation and token overhead. I would still keep the human approval step: simplifying who gathers facts must not let an agent spend the $2,400. Ticket 101 benefits more from separate inventory and accounting checks because an invoice block, stock shortfall, and two different cash outflows interact. Ticket 103 also benefits from independent price and stock checks, because its unresolved margin policy and vendor match are exactly where one confident agent might otherwise fill gaps with guesses.

Gaps

Three problems this toolset cannot safely solve yet

1 · Confirm a delivery and receive the goods into stock

Ticket 101 ended with an approved one-tee purchase order, but the Actual section explicitly says receipt is not modeled; vendor lead time alone cannot establish shipment or arrival. I would add purchase-order status, shipment tracking, and a human-confirmed record_goods_received tool that updates inventory by SKU and size, then add a Purchasing/Receiving agent to reconcile ordered versus received quantities.

2 · Quote a real bulk discount and commit to the hoodie source

Ticket 103 exposed two missing facts: no recorded discount authority and no exact Bulldog-to-hoodie SKU match. I would need a discount-policy/approval table and lookup tools for authorized tiers, plus an exact vendor catalog and product mapping with current cost and lead time. A Pricing agent could calculate a policy-compliant offer with Accounting; a Sourcing agent could verify the SKU and supply. Even then, the $264 cost estimate for the 12-unit shortfall exceeds the actual $152 ending checking balance, so the purchase must stay blocked unless an approved funding decision changes available cash.

3 · Process a delivered-item return or exchange

The current app has no sales fulfillment or receipt history, return authorization, inventory reversal, or refund workflow; it also models cash only going out. A return/refund agent would need order and delivery records, a return inspection/status tool that adjusts SKU/size stock, and a human-approved refund tool that writes a linked refund/payment row while enforcing the balance rule. Without those records, the team could not tell whether an item was actually delivered or what should be refunded.
